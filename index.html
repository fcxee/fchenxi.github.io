Research

My research links microbial ecology to global biogeochemistry through the lens of temperature — asking how warming reshapes the organisms that decompose soil carbon, and how those changes feed back to the climate system.

Carbon Dynamics

Temperature Sensitivity of SOC Decomposition

How strongly does decomposition respond to warming — and why does that sensitivity differ across soils, depths, and climates? I combine incubation experiments with Arrhenius-based and enzyme-kinetic frameworks to resolve the drivers of intrinsic Q₁₀ variability at the global scale.

Microbial Ecology

Microbial Carbon Use Efficiency & Stabilization

The proportion of carbon that microbes retain versus respire — carbon use efficiency — controls how much organic matter persists as mineral-associated organic carbon (MAOC). I investigate how warming, substrate quality, and community composition shift CUE, and how homeoviscous adaptation modulates microbial responses over time.

Climate Modeling

Soil Carbon Feedbacks in Earth System Models

A substantial fraction of uncertainty in future SOC projections traces back to how ESMs parameterize temperature sensitivity. Through global data synthesis and sensitivity analysis, I work to quantify that uncertainty and evaluate how alternative model structures alter long-term climate feedback estimates.
