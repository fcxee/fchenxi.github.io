About

Studying the carbon beneath our feet

I am a soil ecologist and biogeochemist whose research centers on how soil organic carbon is decomposed, stabilized, and ultimately lost to the atmosphere. My work spans four modes of inquiry: field measurements in diverse ecosystems, controlled laboratory incubations, process-based modeling, and global-scale data synthesis.

A unifying thread runs through all of it — understanding the temperature sensitivity of soil carbon turnover, and what that sensitivity means for Earth's climate future. Microbial physiology, mineral associations, and organic matter chemistry all shape the answer in ways that current Earth system models have yet to fully capture.

Research

Three interlocking problems

My research program links microbial ecology to global biogeochemistry through the lens of temperature — asking how warming reshapes the organisms that process soil carbon, and how those changes propagate to the climate system.

Carbon Dynamics

Temperature Sensitivity of SOC Decomposition

How strongly does decomposition respond to warming — and why does that sensitivity differ across soils, depths, and climates? I combine incubation experiments with Arrhenius-based and enzyme-kinetic frameworks to resolve the drivers of intrinsic Q₁₀ variability at the global scale.

Microbial Ecology

Microbial Carbon Use Efficiency & Stabilization

The proportion of carbon that microbes retain versus respire — carbon use efficiency — controls how much organic matter persists as mineral-associated organic carbon (MAOC). I investigate how warming, substrate quality, and community composition shift CUE, and how homeoviscous adaptation modulates microbial responses over time.

Climate Modeling

Soil Carbon Feedbacks in Earth System Models

A substantial fraction of uncertainty in future SOC projections traces back to how ESMs parameterize temperature sensitivity. Through global data synthesis and sensitivity analysis, I work to quantify that uncertainty and evaluate how alternative model structures alter long-term climate feedback estimates.

Manuscript in preparation

Intrinsic Temperature Sensitivity of Soil Organic Carbon Mineralization Across Global Ecosystems: Patterns, Drivers, and Implications for Climate Feedback

A global synthesis examining how ecosystem type, soil properties, and microbial community structure govern the intrinsic temperature sensitivity of SOC mineralization. Integrates the Y-A-S framework, CUE dynamics, MAOC formation, and homeoviscous adaptation alongside evidence on biochar amendments and management strategies for SOC preservation.

Synthesis

Attributing SOC Projection Uncertainty to ESM Temperature Sensitivity Functions

A focused literature analysis quantifying what fraction of inter-model spread in soil carbon projections originates in temperature sensitivity parameterizations. Key inputs: Todd-Brown et al. (2013–2018), Huang et al. (2018) Sobol sensitivity indices, and Varney et al. (2020, 2024).
